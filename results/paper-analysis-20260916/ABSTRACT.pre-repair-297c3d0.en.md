# Abstract

Large language model agents rely on external feedback to test hypotheses, retrieve information, and verify conclusions. When feedback is costly, capability depends on which observations agents acquire as well as how they reason over them. EXPGYM evaluates this dependence using pre-cached feedback and deterministic surrogates for hyperparameter tuning, multi-hop search, and evidence audit under three feedback regimes. Across six models, tightening the budget reduces search F1 and exact evidence-set accuracy for every model, while the model with the highest observed mean changes in five of the seven evaluation dimensions with complete six-model comparisons. Free-feedback selection can therefore incur deployment regret: on the Adult tuning task, choosing GPT, the unique Free leader, costs 15.22 Gap points under Tight relative to Gemini among the same six fully observed candidates. Trajectory analysis reveals that query diversity need not produce new observations, stopping need not imply successful completion, and correct labels can conceal incomplete evidence. For hypotheses requiring nonempty evidence, label accuracy remains approximately unchanged, whereas exact evidence-set accuracy falls from 78.19% to 44.78%. POOLACT addresses acquisition across parallel agents by sharing observations and exploration state before action selection. With four agents and matched per-agent budgets, it improves Tight search over independent rollouts for all six models, with a median gain of 7.01 F1 percentage points, and improves audit evidence accuracy over both independent and cache-sharing baselines in all twelve model–budget settings. Audit trajectories associate these gains with broader verification coverage rather than simply more direct feedback calls. These descriptive results connect budget-aware evaluation, model selection, and multi-agent coordination through the acquisition and use of evidence.

The completed report includes 16 previously missing Gemini experiments, combining historical Sub2 results with OpenRouter backfills. Original adopted scores and scoring rules are retained; the provider change is not an isolated causal comparison. A separate code review identified issues in answer extraction and vote acceptance; diagnostic rescoring has not been incorporated, so these results remain conditional on the historical implementation. HPO behavioral analyses retain their original 480-trajectory sample, while completed HPO score comparisons use all 486 trajectories.
